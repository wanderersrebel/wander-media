What we process and why

We use personal data where necessary for our legitimate interests in operating and securing the channels, checking publication results, preventing duplicate uploads and responding to ordinary enquiries. Where processing is necessary to perform a contract with you, or to take steps you request before entering into one, we rely on contractual necessity. We process rights requests where needed to meet our legal obligations. We do not use uploader data for advertising and we do not sell it.

International transfers

Google, Meta and Telegram may process information outside the UK.

Google's Cloud Data Processing Addendum applies the UK standard contractual clauses, with the UK supplementary terms, to restricted transfers. Meta's Platform Terms incorporate the UK Addendum, which is deemed executed between us, for UK data. Telegram stores UK and EEA account data in the Netherlands, which the UK's adequacy regulations cover.

For each provider's own arrangements see Google's transfer frameworks, Meta's privacy policy and Telegram's privacy policy. We maintain a transfer risk assessment for the transfers that rely on a safeguard, and we can confirm which safeguard applies to a specific transfer if you ask.

Google and YouTube data

The uploader uses YouTube API Services. It uses Google authorisation to access the connected YouTube channel and submit videos to YouTube. The channel checks read the channel ID and, in the Travel uploader, the channel title. Uploads can include a title, description, tags, category, visibility, scheduled publication time and made-for-kids setting. The WanderHouse workflow also checks the uploaded video's channel, visibility and processing status.

Google shows the permissions requested when you connect an account. The uploaders read Google authorisation credentials from files on the publishing computer and use a refresh token to obtain access when needed. YouTube receives the video and publishing details. Google Privacy Policy

Other services

Mimi and WanderHouse send source videos and publishing details directly to Facebook and Instagram when those destinations are selected. Travel and Mimi also use Google Drive and Google Sheets to stage finished videos and register publishing results.

Wander Media holds exported cloud publishing workflow definitions for these channels. They are not currently running and perform no automated publishing.

Wander Media uses Telegram to send publishing status notifications to the operator. Depending on the job, a notification can include a video title, publishing slot or link.

Publishing records

Wander Media keeps local publishing records to track uploads, investigate uncertain results and avoid publishing the same video twice. Mimi records a fingerprint we generate from the video file, the platform, the date and the returned video ID. WanderHouse records platform outcomes, returned IDs and links. Travel records file details, upload outcomes and returned YouTube video IDs. Video IDs and links can also appear in upload logs and archived filenames.

How long we keep information

We assess how long locally generated records are needed by considering whether an upload outcome is still unresolved, whether a record is still needed to prevent a duplicate, and whether a copy is needed for recovery. There is no automatic deletion schedule at present: records are deleted when you ask us to remove them, or when we decide they are no longer needed. We are developing automated retention and deletion safeguards.

YouTube requires ordinary authorised API data to be refreshed or deleted within 30 calendar days while authorisation remains active. That requirement also covers data copied into receipts, logs, archived filenames and backups. A receipt's purpose does not change the origin of a YouTube video ID or a link derived from it.

Encrypted backup copies are kept on Google Drive and on the publishing computer so the channels can be restored after a failure. Backups are refreshed as they are replaced and are deleted when they are no longer needed for recovery.

Disconnecting and requesting deletion

Remove the uploader's access in Google Account connections. To ask Wander Media to disconnect the uploader or delete stored data, email wandererswindow@gmail.com with the affected channel so we can identify the request. We handle requests under Albert's direction, covering affected credentials and data in live records and any copies, including backups.

Deletion is handled manually today. On receiving your request we confirm the affected channel, revoke any remaining authorisation, remove the local publishing records and logs for that channel, and delete the corresponding backup copies, then confirm to you what was removed.

YouTube requires deletion as soon as possible and within seven calendar days for a deletion request or revocation through the app's own mechanism, and immediate programmatic revocation of the token. For revocation made through Google Account connections, YouTube requires periodic authorisation checks and deletion as soon as possible within 30 calendar days of revocation. These are requirements we work to. Retention and deletion are currently handled manually, and we are developing automated safeguards to support them.

Local duplicate-prevention records are based on a fingerprint we generate from each video file.

Removing the uploader's copies leaves the video on YouTube. To remove the video, use YouTube Studio or another authorised tool that supports deletion on YouTube.
